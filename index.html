THE PROBLEM

People cross. Cars turn.
Both hope the other one stops.

At a busy Indian junction, the most dangerous moments are not crashes — they are the near-misses nobody records. A pedestrian still on the zebra when the light turns. A two-wheeler squeezed into the junction mouth, right where a car is about to turn. Nothing is written down, so nothing gets fixed. RakshaPath rebuilds that junction as a living 3D model, counts every near-miss, and lets you test three small fixes that real junctions can actually adopt.

The crossing is the conflict

People on foot cross exactly where vehicles turn. When the walking phase is short, slower walkers are still on the road when traffic gets the green — and drivers brake hard, or don't.

Two-wheelers squeeze to the front

In queued traffic, riders filter through gaps and wait inside the junction mouth — invisible to a driver who is about to turn across their path.

Nobody can see the pattern

A near-miss leaves no report and no data. Which crossing is worst? Which small change helps most? Without a record, a junction stays dangerous by default. This model keeps the record.

HOW THIS MODEL WORKS

Simple rules, stated plainly

  • Left-hand traffic, one fixed signal cycle: north–south green, amber, a pedestrian phase on all four crossings, then east–west.
  • Drivers brake for people in their path. If a driver has to brake hard on a green light because someone is still crossing, the model records a near-miss and marks the spot.
  • Some riders filter. In a queue of two or more at a red light, a two-wheeler may squeeze to the front. Without a stop box it waits inside the junction mouth — and turning traffic sweeps past it.
  • The school corner matters. Groups of children cross near the school on the north-east corner, and they do not always wait for the lights.
  • Every fix is mechanical, not magic. The island shortens each crossing stage. The stop box keeps riders visible and in line. Rumble strips lower approach speeds, so braking stays gentle.

Honest note: this is a teaching model with simple rules. It does not use real traffic data, and it does not predict what any real junction will do. It exists so the pattern of near-misses — and the effect of small fixes — can be seen, counted and argued about with evidence.
